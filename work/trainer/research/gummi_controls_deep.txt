Gummi controls, weapons and editor: findings for v0.8
====================================================
Unpublished work. These additions are separate from the delivered v0.7.1.
No live gameplay or UI actions were performed.

Useful controls completed
-------------------------
The main ship has an aggregated runtime state built from its installed parts.
Three native adders change its movement, roll and lock-on bonuses. The trainer
converts the requested final value to a checked delta and calls the appropriate
adder once. These changes last until the game recalculates the loadout or replaces
the ship.

Movement bonus affects lateral speed, acceleration and bank-angle smoothing.
It is not a forward route-speed setting. Its effective factor is clamped between
0.1 and3; negative bonuses first pass through a loaded handling coefficient.

Roll bonus changes the initial roll impulse, with an effective factor from0 to3.
The roll countdown, effect and temporary immunity continue through native code.

Lock-on bonus reduces future acquisition-delay reloads. Each of the four native
categories keeps its resource-defined minimum, and already-running timers keep
their current value.

Special-weapon charge is an installed combo system with stock, partial progress,
five possible main-ship stages and optional companion stages. Adding charge uses
the game's normal multiplier, integer conversion and stock conversion. It does
not create missing weapons or fire an attack. Native stock caps at9; saturation
keeps progress100 and a full flag until activation. Readouts expose stock, progress,
phase, remaining roll time and effective movement/roll factors.

Exact slots, ranges, ABI, resource identities, lifecycle gates and integration
entry points are documented in gummi_controls_contract.txt. The English feature
catalog is gummi_extra_features.json. All edits act once; no persistent hook,
automatic replay, retained ship pointer or automatic restoration is installed.

Other concrete findings
------------------------
The inspected GUN update has separate fire-request, disabled and burst flags.
Burst interval/countdown are at128/132; ordinary firing delay/countdown are at
136/140. Native delay setter1F2680 changes both the base and remaining delay.
Burst setter1F2620 also controls count and reset behavior; nonpositive intervals
can change how many projectiles are emitted in an update.

Gun lifetime is more involved than a single current-player pointer. A global list
uses packed next160, while part-owned chains use next164. Part destruction unlinks
and destroys its guns. Some part types redirect ownership. A future per-gun
controller needs complete current-ship/part/gun selection and lifetime validation.

DRAIN has its own staged behavior: charge/wait, projectile absorption and firing,
then return to ready. This is separate from a demonstrated universal energy or
overheat value. No universal overheat toggle is claimed by this research.

The editor's HISTORY object holds two groups of up to1000 item records, each
176 bytes. These are before/after groups, not1000 undo steps. Undo and redo remove
one group's live objects and recreate the other group's objects through the menu
allocator and WORKAREA. Their safe use requires editor-specific ownership and
retained-handle checks.

Round5 correction (working research source only):27EB70 is the Undo helper,
called by269B50 before it decrements the WORKAREA history cursor.27EB30 is
the Redo helper, called by265580 after it increments the cursor. The earlier
JSON labels were reversed; the group0/group1 behavior was already accurate.
See gummi_round5_deep.json and gummi_round5_history_correction.json for the
caller/snapshot evidence. No released trainer action uses these helpers; the
historical0.9 package and Source.zip remain unchanged.

Editor capacity is recomputed from native base limits, mode, companion count and
save-backed upgrades170/171/175. Changing a displayed capacity alone would not
establish that placement and save validation accept the design.
